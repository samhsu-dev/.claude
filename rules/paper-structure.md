# Paper Structure

## Opening

- The subject of the first sentence of the abstract and of the introduction is the paper's central artifact or domain, named by the title's main noun.
- A technology, language, or implementation detail is the subject of the opening sentence only when the title names it.
- The opening states the paper's purpose. Never open with why the field matters.

## Abstract and Introduction

- The abstract has four sentences in order: (1) the problem, (2) why it is hard, (3) the approach, (4) the result.
- The introduction holds the problem description and the contribution list, at most one page. No background, related work, or method detail.
- The introduction states (1) the contribution, (2) the new result and how it differs from prior work, (3) the evidence for the result.
- Related work follows the approach section.
- The title (about 10 words), the abstract (about 100 words), the introduction (about 1000 words), and the body state the same contribution. Each is readable alone.

## Contributions

- Each contribution is one refutable sentence with a forward reference to its evidence: "We show X satisfies property Y (Section N)". "We describe X" is no contribution.
- The contribution list states whether each contribution is novel (a new observation) or incremental (an improvement over named prior work).

## Conclusions

- The conclusion states only implications, conjectures, open problems, and insights that depend on the whole paper.
- Never summarize the paper in the conclusion.
